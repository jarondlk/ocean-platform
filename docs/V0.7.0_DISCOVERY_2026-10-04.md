## Requirements and literature

[Issue #89](https://github.com/jarondlk/ocean-platform/issues/89),
“Notes on system requirements for the presentation,” contains six questions in
three families: a regional time series and SST contrast; a best-covered monthly
spatial distribution and temperature comparison; and matched-area/season changes
between 2020 and 2023 followed through 2021–2022. Detection frequency explicitly
means the proportion of samples with a detection. Coverage, sampled non-detection,
unsampled areas and traceability are requirements. Miyagi, sardine and the dates
are examples that may be adjusted to available data.

The [feedback comment](https://github.com/jarondlk/ocean-platform/issues/89#issuecomment-5945141912)
links these research references:

| Reference | Planning relevance and limits |
| --- | --- |
| [Regional coastal fish niche axes](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-31307-4) | The paper uses a 2017 survey, species-distribution models and alternative environmental covariates. Its discussion distinguishes field measurements from satellite-derived environmental summaries and considers longer environmental windows. It informs explicit covariates and careful interpretation; v0.7.0 does not reproduce its model or treat its results as observations in OCEAN. |
| [Seasonal latitudinal diversity gradients](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-52959-w) | The methods distinguish surveys, seasons, taxonomic resolution and sequencing methods; the discussion addresses uneven sampling, spatial autocorrelation, detectability and transient fish. The abstract and methods give different survey totals, so do not reuse a paper headline count as OCEAN's denominator. Genus-level findings do not establish a species-level sardine result. |
| [High-frequency coastal fish dataset](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-026-08246-4) | The publisher abstract describes annual/monthly/biweekly designs during January 2018–March 2019. It reinforces explicit cadence and sampling units. The publisher's full methods/PDF could not be fetched in this session; an author-preprint download hit a robot challenge. Detailed methodological reuse needs the full published version and supplements. This dataset does not itself establish 2020–2023 coverage. |

## Code foundations and gaps

- `db/models.py` has source occurrences, assays, optional `physical_sample_id`
  and `identity_status`. Catalogue normalization in `preprocessing/anemone.py`
  deliberately writes unresolved physical identity and marks worldmesh-derived
  coordinates `provider_grid_unconfirmed`.
- The published catalogue record describes 3,498 source occurrences/assays,
  343 explicit controls and 3,155 unknown classifications. Zero explicitly
  environmental records were accepted in the retained publication. This is
  a dated baseline, not a fresh production census.
- `preprocessing/edna_eligibility.py` excludes unknown/control, incomplete
  protocol and unavailable method inputs. `api/classification_review_service.py`
  already provides evidence-bound review; implementation must extend/reuse it,
  not infer environmental status from names or coordinates.
- `preprocessing/edna_recipe.py` defines strict v1 recipes limited to 200
  environmental assays. `ingestion/edna_analysis_bundle.py` reads a consistent
  PostgreSQL snapshot, caps sample/control-inclusive assay reads at 1,000 and
  validates immutable artifacts. Simply raising a limit would not implement
  sample-frequency denominators or scalable regional analyses.
- `preprocessing/edna_analysis.py` computes method-separated composition,
  diversity and assay-pair turnover. It does not implement the six required
  detection-frequency workflows.
- `retrieval/edna_environment_linker.py` already enforces domains, coordinate
  uncertainty, time/depth compatibility, quality, footprint and coverage. It
  correctly rejects unconfirmed provider grids. A new reviewed area-based SST
  comparison must retain this protection and distinguish surface context from
  collocated bulk-water measurements.
- `preprocessing/remote_sensing.py` uses filename times, nearest-point extraction
  and broad regional summaries. The normalized SST tables have no product-aware
  per-area historical panel. Neither they nor the current `/data/sst` response
  establish a complete satellite analysis product/quality/provenance contract.
- `api/edna_analysis_routes.py`, registered artifact storage, result IDs and
  provenance/export routes are reusable. Their table sets and v1 codec are
  fixed; adding new tables requires a versioned reader and API/navigation work.
- Existing analysis-to-chat scope matching rejects taxon subfilters and differing
  cohort settings; context excerpts contain only the first five rows. v0.7.0
  needs exact result selection, not statistics inferred from these excerpts.
- `frontend/components/EdnaAnalysisView.tsx` primarily browses existing tables.
  The existing SVG chart approach is reusable, but there is no linked
  detection-frequency map/time-series workspace.

## Local SST inspection

A filesystem census found **1,848 NetCDF files** under `onagawa_sst_subset/`.
All file names have a parseable timestamp, spanning **2025-12-05 00:00 through
2026-02-26 23:00**. These filename dates have no overlap with 2020–2023.
The earliest/latest files' metadata and coordinate arrays were opened:

- Title: `Product name: JCOPE-T DA Best Estimate`.
- Latitude approximately 38.01389–38.98611; longitude 141.01389–141.98611.
- 36 × 36 grid, one time and one surface depth; SST unit `degC`.
- The recorded time unit is hours since 2000-01-01; filename/time agreement
  and full-file quality need validation during SST preparation.

The [JAXA guide](https://www.eorc.jaxa.jp/ptree/userguide.html) separates Himawari
geophysical SST from JCOPE-T model products. The inspected files therefore cannot
be presented as direct satellite SST observations. The title was checked on two
files, not every file; the acquisition inventory must verify every product/version.

## Archived ANEMONE coverage probes

All 3,498 `sample.tsv.xz` metadata tables in the retained local 2026-09-17 archive
were read without changing them. These are source-metadata coverage probes,
not approved environmental cohorts or deduplicated physical samples.

For an **unreviewed** bounding box 38–39°N / 141–142°E, UTC 2020–2023:

| Year | Source occurrences | Distinct reported names | Reported grid values | Collection months |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 2020 | 36 | 36 | 3 | January–December |
| 2021 | 32 | 32 | 6 | January–December |
| 2022 | 40 | 33 | 6 | January–December |
| 2023 | 10 | 10 | 1 | January–May |
| Total | 118 | 111 | 8 | Uneven across years |

This box is not a reviewed Miyagi coastline boundary. Reported names are not
physical-sample IDs, and grid values are not confirmed stations. In this box,
2023 cannot support a broad multiple-area comparison across all seasons.

Across the entire archive, raw 2023 metadata with worldmesh values has its largest
monthly count of reported cells in **May: 36**, followed by March: 32 and April: 31.
These are candidate discovery results only. The required best-covered month must
be recomputed after eligibility, identity, area and protocol decisions. It must
not be hard-coded to May or silently expanded from Miyagi to nationwide scope.

## Consequence for the implementation order

First establish a reviewed analysis unit, eligible cohort, geographic registry
and actual historical satellite SST coverage. Then compute all six results
deterministically, publish their exact inputs/results, and expose those same
results in Chat, maps, time series and downloads. Synthetic fixtures can validate
the implementation while provider/data decisions are outstanding; they cannot
count as real-data demo acceptance.
